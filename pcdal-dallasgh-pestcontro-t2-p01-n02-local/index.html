In2Care® Mosquito Traps & Autodissemination Larvicide Technology

The innovative In2Care system uses female egg-laying mosquitoes to disperse biological larvicide to surrounding breeding sites. When female mosquitoes enter the trap to lay eggs, they become contaminated with pyriproxyfen (an insect growth regulator) and a fungal entomopathogen, which they distribute to hidden water catchments before dying.

  • Targeted green stations placed in shaded vegetation canopy zones around the property perimeter
  • Autodissemination mechanism allowing mosquitoes to carry biological larvicide to concealed outdoor puddles

Frequently asked questions

When does peak mosquito season occur in Dallas County?

Mosquito activity in Dallas typically begins in April as temperatures consistently exceed 50°F, peaks during the hot and humid months of June through September, and remains active until the first freezing cold snap in late November.

How effective are In2Care mosquito stations compared to backpack misting?

Backpack misting delivers immediate 21 to 30 day adult knockdown on treated foliage, while In2Care stations provide sustained 24/7 population reduction by turning mosquitoes into carriers that eliminate surrounding breeding sites. Combining both methods yields the most comprehensive yard protection.

Does mosquito spraying harm honeybees and beneficial pollinators?

Responsible IPM technicians apply barrier treatments early in the morning or at dusk when bees are not foraging, direct sprays strictly onto shaded non-flowering foliage where mosquitoes harbor, and completely avoid blooming flower beds.
